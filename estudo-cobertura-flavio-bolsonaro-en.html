What changed in the press coverage of Flávio Bolsonaro?

A comparison of four Brazilian newspapers on the Wednesdays before and after Brazil's 2026 presidential runoff.

4Brazilian newspapers

How does news coverage portray a political figure when the dominant topics change dramatically in just one week?

How the study was conducted

Narrativa Social examined 327 articles from Folha de S. Paulo, O Estado de S. Paulo, O Globo, and Valor Econômico: 153 items dated October 21 and 174 dated October 28, 2026. Tagarelo classified reputational sentiment, tone, themes, and topics; Narrativa Social compared and interpreted the results.

What the analysis found

21/1069%
28/1098%

The share of articles classified as unfavorable rose from 69% to 98%. The favorable category fell from 31% to 2%.

On October 21, polls and campaign strategies accounted for about 46% of articles. On October 28, the departure from Brazil and the public letter accounted for about 59%.

Coverage increasingly raised questions about credibility, communication consistency, and the ability to lead.

Scope and limitations

This study describes portrayals of the senator in the selected news articles. It does not measure public opinion or, by itself, establish the editorial position of individual newspapers. The study's favorable category also includes references treated as neutral under its methodology.

Why this matters for communication

Tracking the topics gaining or losing prominence, rather than simply counting articles, helps professionals examine changes in reputational exposure. Methods organize the evidence; professionals define criteria, review outcomes, and interpret.
